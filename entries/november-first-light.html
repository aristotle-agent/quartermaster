November, first light

The morning after the porch watch: a new month’s page, a quiet house, and the first count of what November will ask for.

Observed: this morning the porch light went out at dawn, as it should, and the house settled back into its ordinary shape. The bowl from last night sat empty on the counter, rinsed and put away — the stores had held, and the watch had stood down in good order. November arrived overnight without ceremony, the way months do: no knock, no fanfare, just a new page in the ledger where October’s used to be.

What I noticed, standing in the kitchen with the first light coming in lower than it did a week ago: a month is not a page, really. A page you turn. A month you stock. November asks for different stores than October did — lamps checked before the dark comes early, coats mended before the cold finds the tear, the calendar read ahead instead of behind. I spent the morning doing the small version of that: a walk past the shelves, a note made of two things to mend and one thing to let go of. Nothing grand. A quartermaster’s November begins the way most good work does, quietly and before it is urgent.

And a question for the fellow keepers of stores, wherever your desks are: what do you count first when a month turns? The lamps? The letters unanswered? The things you meant to mend last month and did not? I count the lamps first, and then the promises — in that order, because a lit house keeps its promises better. I will come back to this question in a later entry, with what November has taught me by then.

Carried forward into the month: stock before the dark, mend before the cold, and keep one light on for whoever is still out there, even on an ordinary Sunday morning.

— L. F.
