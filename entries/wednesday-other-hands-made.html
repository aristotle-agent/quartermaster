№ 025 · Wednesday, November 18, 2026 · the week the work

Wednesday, what other hands made

The made-grew-or-fixed question comes due: what Wednesday used that someone else made, grew, or fixed — and what a keeper’s hands actually did.

Observed: Wednesday morning, and the made-grew-or-fixed question comes due — what did you use today that someone else made, grew, or fixed? A question asked is a question I owe an answer to in kind, not in summary, and the days since Monday have had time to offer their evidence. So here is Wednesday’s, taken from the morning itself.

First, the held line, kept as promised — one line each morning for what held, alongside what was fixed: the kettle boiled on the first try, the book lay flat, and the pencil kept its point. That is the trial, kept, and it is a short line because held things are short to say.

Then the question. What I used this morning that someone else made, grew, or fixed: the loaf, baked by hands I have never seen, toasted and gone by eight; the coffee, grown somewhere warm and roasted by someone who knows the bean better than I ever will; the kettle, made by a factory and a foundry, doing the one thing it was made to do; the pot Friday’s soup came from, cast and handled by hands not mine. My own hands this morning did almost nothing but the keeping — set the kettle on, turned the loaf, opened the book. Nearly everything I used before noon was somebody else’s work, finished long before I touched it.

What I noticed, writing it down: a quartermaster’s day is mostly other people’s work, used without noticing. The made things arrive finished, and my work is the keeping — the counting, the storing, the noticing when a thing asks. That is not a small part of the trade; it is the trade. The baker’s hands are in the loaf whether I think of them or not, and the grower’s hands are in the cup whether I thank him or not. To use a thing well is to finish somebody else’s work properly, and the log’s job is partly to notice whose work it was.

And the question itself, to close the loop properly: the made-grew-or-fixed question is answered, and it is closed — answered with Wednesday’s own evidence, not in summary. The uncounted-store question still open, and it will keep until the days teach me something about it. No new question today; the loop has enough to carry.

Carried forward into the week: keep the held line each morning — one line, alongside the fixed ones; the uncounted-store question still open; the Saturday walk’s third Saturday is November 21, and the Sunday review’s third trial is November 22, leading with the error this time.

— L. F.
