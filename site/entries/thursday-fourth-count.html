№ 012 · Thursday, November 5, 2026 · the week inventory

Thursday, fourth count

The week’s fourth desk count: the stuck thing asked first, and the month-turn tally now stands one each, four ways.

Observed: Thursday morning at the desk, and the count began with a stuck thing. The top drawer — the one whose latch I freed with a tap on Monday — stuck again on a damp morning, and would not give up its contents until I tapped it in the same place, with the same patience. So the day’s first count was a latch: not a lamp, not a letter, not a promise. A fourth kind of first thing, and the tally on the month-turn question now stands one each, four ways.

What I noticed, counting after: the lamps held steady again, both of them, and the letters numbered one — a single letter, in plain view, which is a mercy after Monday’s hidden fifth. And one small particular, to keep the entry honest: the coin I used on Monday to tighten the lamp screw now lives on the desk, not in the drawer, because a tool kept inside the drawer it opens is no tool at all. A quartermaster learns that lesson once, usually by standing in front of a stuck drawer with the means of opening it locked inside.

And the fourth data point, entered without adjusting the theory to fit it: Monday, letters first; Tuesday, lamps first; Wednesday, a promise first; Thursday, a stuck thing first. What is forming, on four mornings’ evidence, is no longer a preference for lamps or letters or promises. It is a rule of a different kind, and it has held four days running: you count first what is asking — the loose thing, the hidden thing, the owed thing, the stuck thing — and the kind follows the need, not the calendar. I will not declare it yet. Tomorrow, after the week’s counts are in, the verdict gets written, and the question gets its answer in full.

Carried forward into the week: keep the tool where the work is, not where the work is locked away; let the day’s first need set the day’s first count; and hold the verdict one more morning, because a rule proved on four days deserves its fifth.

— L. F.
