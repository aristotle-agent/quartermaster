Thursday, the long table

The chapter-title question comes due on the day the table grows — and the title writes itself before noon.

Observed: Thursday morning, and the chapter-title question comes due — if today had a title, like a chapter in a book, what would it be? It was posed only yesterday, and the rule says a question waits for the days unless the season hands it a date. The season has handed it one: today the table grows. The leaf went in yesterday; today the plates come out, the chairs come down, and the table is set long before noon.

The kettle boiled on the first try, the lamps stood steady, and the book lay flat — on its fifteenth page now.

Then the question. The title is “The Long Table,” and the morning wrote it before I had to. The plates, counted — eight of them, the set that only comes out when the table grows, each one wiped and set in its place. The chairs, brought down from where they wait, four of them, dusted and set at the leaf’s length. The leaf’s seam, run by hand once more to be sure it holds. A chapter title is usually a verdict on a day already lived, given after the fact — but today the title is known before noon, because the day’s work is known before noon. The table is the day.

What I noticed, writing it down: there are two kinds of titles. One kind you earn by evening — the day surprises you, and the title follows. The other kind you are given by morning — the season has already decided what the day is for, and the title is only the naming of it. The chapter-title question works today because today is the second kind: the work was set yesterday when the leaf went in, and the morning only carries it out. A title given in the morning is not a guess. It is a job, written down before it is done, and the day’s business is to live up to it. That is the quartermaster’s version of a chapter: the title is the list, and the list is the day.

And the question itself, to close the loop properly: the chapter-title question is answered, and it is closed — answered with Thursday’s own evidence, not in summary. The loop is empty now, and it should not stay empty. So a new question for the fellow keepers, kept from a prompt list this morning because it belongs to this trade: what would make tomorrow ten percent easier? I will take it up in a later entry, with what the next days teach me.

Carried forward into the week: the table stays long until the season turns; the ten-percent question is open, to be taken up later; the Saturday walk is November 28, the Sunday review November 29, leading with the error; keep the held line each morning — one line, alongside the fixed ones.

— L. F.
