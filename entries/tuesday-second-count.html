№ 010 · Tuesday, November 3, 2026 · the week inventory

Tuesday, second count

The week’s second desk count: lamps first this time, a loose bulb tightened by hand — and the month-turn tally now stands one to one.

Observed: Tuesday morning I set out to test Monday on purpose — lamps before letters, in the order I claimed in № 008, rather than the order Monday actually took. The desk lamp burned steady. The hall lamp did not: its bulb had worked loose in the socket, and a turn by hand, no tool and no replacement, put it right. That is a mending, not a repair, and the distinction matters to a quartermaster. A repair replaces what is broken. A mending restores what is only loose, and it costs nothing but attention paid in time.

What I noticed, counting second, as the day’s bookends would have it — first thing lamps, last thing the letters: there were three letters, and none of them hiding. A clean count, for once, with nothing tucked behind anything else. And a small particular to keep the entry honest: a pencil worn down to a stub, retired not to the bin but to the jar of stubs, because a stub still writes a short note, and most notes worth writing are short.

And the second data point on the month-turn question, entered without adjusting the theory to fit it: Monday, letters first; Tuesday, lamps first. The tally stands one to one. What is forming, on two days’ evidence only, is not a fixed order at all. Monday a letter was hiding, so letters asked first. Tuesday a bulb was loose, so lamps asked first. Perhaps you count first what is asking — the loose thing, the hidden thing — and the kind follows the need, not the calendar. I will not declare that yet. I will settle the question on Friday, after the week’s counts are in, rather than on two mornings’ say-so.

Carried forward into the week: tighten what is loose while it is still only loose, keep the stubs, and let the day’s first need set the day’s first count. A clean ledger is not one without crossings-out. It is one where every crossing-out can be read — and, this week, one where the counts are allowed to argue with the theory until Friday.

— L. F.
