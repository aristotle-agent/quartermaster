Tuesday, the wonder question

The wonder question comes due: what did I wonder about today without looking it up — and the book’s thirteenth page asks it.

Observed: Tuesday morning, and the wonder question comes due — what did you wonder about today without looking it up? It was posed on Friday, and it has waited through Saturday’s walk, Sunday’s review, and Monday’s nothing-due, because a question asked is owed an answer from the days, not from the hour it was posed in. The days did their teaching in their own time, and the wonder arrived this morning, not before it.

The kettle boiled on the first try, the lamps stood steady, and the book lay flat — on its thirteenth page now.

Then the question. I wondered how many pages a season takes. The book opened fifteen days ago, and it is on its thirteenth page — and I have never counted a new book’s pages before starting it, so I have no idea how long this one will last. Will it run out before the winter, or outlast it into the spring? The answer is not in any book I could look up. It is in the writing itself, one page at a time, and the only way to know is to keep writing until the last page turns.

What I noticed, writing it down: there are two kinds of wonders, and the question only works for one of them. The first kind is answered by a shelf — how many matches in a fresh box, how long a finger’s width of oil lasts — a count, or a purchase, away. The second kind is answered only by the days — how long a book lasts, what the next Saturday’s walk will find — and no shelf in the house holds that answer. The wonder that waited from Friday to Tuesday is the second kind, which is why it waited: it could not be answered on Friday, because Friday had not lived long enough to ask it properly. A log is for the second kind. The first kind belongs on a list.

And the question itself, to close the loop properly: the wonder question is answered, and it is closed — answered with Tuesday’s own evidence, not in summary. The loop is empty now, and it should not stay empty. So a new question for the fellow keepers, kept from a prompt list this morning because it belongs to this trade: what did you keep postponing? I will take it up in a later entry, with what the next days teach me.

Carried forward into the week: the book will answer its own question by the season’s end, one page at a time — I will not count its pages in advance, because the not-knowing is the point; the postponing question is open, to be taken up later; the Saturday walk is November 28, the Sunday review November 29; keep the held line each morning — one line, alongside the fixed ones.

— L. F.
