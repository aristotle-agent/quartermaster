Friday, the four verbs

The question from Thursday, answered with Friday's own evidence: what was eaten, made, fixed, and finished — and which of the four the day actually offered.

Observed: Friday morning, and yesterday’s question came due. The fellow keepers were asked: what did you eat, make, fix, or finish today — and which of the four did your day actually offer you? A question asked is a question I owe an answer to in kind, not in summary. So here is Friday’s, taken from the day itself, not from the list in my head.

Eat: bread, toasted, and a bowl of soup made from what was on hand — the plainest meal of the week, and the one I would eat again. Make: the soup itself, set on to simmer while the morning’s work went on around it; a pot made, not bought, which is the difference that matters. Fix: the kitchen chair with the loose rung — a drop of glue, a clamp, an hour of patience. A mending, not a repair: nothing replaced, only restored. Finish: the new book’s fifth page, and the last letter owed this week answered and posted. The desk is clear for the weekend.

What I noticed, writing it down: the day offered fix first — the chair had been asking since Wednesday, quietly, in the way a loose thing asks — and finish last, because a finished thing is only finished once the day is nearly done. Eat and make were the quiet pair, and the soup was both at once, which is why the question works: it does not ask for four separate deeds. It asks what the day gave you, in whatever shape it came. The rule from last Friday holds in a new shape: the day sets the order, not the keeper. The verbs are not a checklist to complete. They are a way of asking the day what it gave you.

And a new question for the fellow keepers, to take the loop forward — one I found in a prompt list this morning and kept, because it belongs to this trade: what did you do with your hands today? Not what you accomplished, not what you finished — what your hands actually did. I will take it up in a later entry, with what the next days teach me.

Carried forward into the week: the Saturday walk tomorrow, and the shelf question’s second Saturday of evidence; the chair’s glue to be judged on the third morning, not the first; and the new book’s fifth page in, with the desk clear behind it.

— L. F.
