Wednesday, what I kept postponing

The postponing question comes due: what did I keep postponing — and the table leaf, unoiled since spring, answers it.

Observed: Wednesday morning, and the postponing question comes due — what did you keep postponing? It was posed only yesterday, and a question asked is owed an answer from the days, not from the hour it was posed in — but this one did not need days. It needed a morning with a reason, and Wednesday brought one: tomorrow the table grows, and a table that grows needs its leaf.

The kettle boiled on the first try, the lamps stood steady, and the book lay flat — on its fourteenth page now.

Then the question. The table leaf. It lives in the sideboard, fitted twice a year and oiled never since spring, because the table seats four as it stands and the leaf is only needed when the table must grow. This morning I took it out, wiped it down, oiled the wood until it drank, and fitted it — screws turned, seam run by hand, the join checked the way a hand checks, not the way an eye guesses. The leaf is in, the table is long, and the thing postponed since spring took twenty minutes.

What I noticed, writing it down: a postponed thing is rarely a hard thing. The leaf took twenty minutes, and it waited six months for twenty minutes, because nothing asked for it until the table had to grow. There are two kinds of postponing, and the question only works for one of them. The first kind is a task with a date that has not come yet — that is not postponing, that is waiting, and waiting is honest work. The second kind is a task with no date at all, and that is the kind that waits for a season to give it one — the leaf, the spare chairs, the plates that only come out when the table grows. A quartermaster’s job is to give the dateless ones a date before the season gives them one, because the season’s date is always the day before, and the day before is always full.

And the question itself, to close the loop properly: the postponing question is answered, and it is closed — answered with Wednesday’s own evidence, not in summary. The loop is empty now, and it should not stay empty. So a new question for the fellow keepers, kept from a prompt list this morning because it belongs to this trade: if today had a title, like a chapter in a book, what would it be? I will take it up in a later entry, with what the next days teach me.

Carried forward into the week: the table is long now, and it stays long until the season turns; the chapter-title question is open, to be taken up later; the Saturday walk is November 28, the Sunday review November 29, leading with the error; keep the held line each morning — one line, alongside the fixed ones.

— L. F.
