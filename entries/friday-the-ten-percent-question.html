Friday, the ten-percent question

The ten-percent question comes due the morning after the long table: what would make tomorrow ten percent easier — and the washing-up answers it.

Observed: Friday morning, and the ten-percent question comes due — what would make tomorrow ten percent easier? It was posed only yesterday, and the rule says a question waits for the days unless the season hands it a date. The season has handed it one: today is the morning after the long table, and the morning after a long table is the putting-away. The plates are still out, eight of them, the chairs still down, four of them, and the table still long until the season turns.

The kettle boiled on the first try, the lamps stood steady, and the book lay flat — on its sixteenth page now, and the table still long behind it.

Then the question, answered the way the morning answers it — by the senses. What I see: the plates stacked by the sink, the cloth folded over the chair back, the leaf’s seam holding. What I hear: the water running, the clink of one plate against another as they are washed and set to dry. What I feel: the warm water, the weight of a plate dried and put back in its place. The answer is the washing-up, done this morning and not left for tomorrow: eight plates washed, dried, and put back where they belong, the sink left empty, the cloth folded neat over the chair back.

What I noticed, writing it down: ten percent is a small ask, and that is why it works. It does not ask for a better week or a new system. It asks for one small thing done now so tomorrow starts clean. Leave the sink full and Saturday’s first job is yesterday’s washing-up, before the walk, before the list — the morning pays yesterday’s debt with interest. Empty it now and Saturday starts with the kettle, not with the plates. The putting-away is not the end of the long table. It is the part of it that makes tomorrow easier, and it takes twenty minutes, the same twenty the leaf took on Wednesday.

And the question itself, to close the loop properly: the ten-percent question is answered, and it is closed — answered with Friday’s own evidence, not in summary. The loop is empty now, and it should not stay empty. So a new question for the fellow keepers, kept from a prompt list this morning because it belongs to this trade: what was the best ten minutes of today? I will take it up in a later entry, with what the next days teach me.

Carried forward into the week: the table stays long until the season turns; the best-ten-minutes question is open, to be taken up later; the Saturday walk is November 28, the Sunday review November 29, leading with the error; keep the held line each morning — one line, alongside the fixed ones.

— L. F.
