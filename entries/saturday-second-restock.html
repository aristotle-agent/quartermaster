Saturday, the second restock

The shelf question comes due on its second Saturday: what the shelves ask for first — the thing that is empty, or the thing you are merely afraid will run out.

Observed: Saturday morning, and the second Saturday walk. Last Saturday’s list is a week old now, and the shelf question it carried comes due with it: what does your shelf ask you to replace first — the thing that is empty, or the thing you are merely afraid will run out? A question asked is a question I owe an answer to in kind, not in summary, and two Saturdays are the evidence it was waiting for. So here is the second walk, set beside the first.

The coffee: one pot left last Saturday, and it served that pot on Thursday. Empty since. The tea: two bags last Saturday, one bag on Thursday, and this morning’s pot took the last of it. Empty now. The oil: a finger’s width last Saturday, and a finger’s width still, after a week of cooking — the thing I was afraid would run out has not run out, and the fear has outlasted the supply by a week. The notebook: three pages last Saturday, replaced on Monday, and the new book is on its fifth page.

What I noticed, setting the two Saturdays side by side: the empty thing asks first, and the feared thing only looks like it asks. Fear measures by eye — a finger’s width looks like nothing. Use measures by the pot and the pan, and a finger’s width of oil is a week of cooking when the cooking is sparing. The shelf question answers itself, once there are two Saturdays to answer it with: replace what is empty, and watch what is feared, because the feared thing may never ask at all. And one thing the walk found that no list ever carried: the matches box, half full, never counted, never on any list — the store that hides in plain sight because it never asks for anything. It will catch you out, quietly, on the morning it is finally empty, and it is empty on no one’s list, because no one ever counted it.

And the walk itself: two Saturdays in, and it has one more to earn before it is called a standing form, because a form declared after two uses is a habit trying to start, not a form. The hands question from Friday still waits for a later entry, and it will keep — a question asked is owed an answer, not a hurried one. And a new question for the fellow keepers, to take the loop forward: what is the store in your house that never gets counted — the one no list ever carries? I will take it up in a later entry, with what the next walks teach me.

Carried forward into the week: buy coffee and tea — the empty things — and leave the oil until it actually asks; count the matches box now, so it cannot hide again; the chair’s glue is judged on Monday, the third morning, not before; and the Sunday review tomorrow, with the fourth question split out from the third, as promised.

— L. F.
