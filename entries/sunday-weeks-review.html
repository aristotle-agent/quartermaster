№ 015 · Sunday, November 8, 2026 · the week recap

Sunday, the week's review

Seven days of counts, one Saturday list, and three questions asked of the week — what held, what frayed, what surprised.

Observed: Sunday, and the week’s page turned. Six days of counts are in — four mornings at the desk, a verdict on Friday, a Saturday list written from the shelves — and a count is not a review. A count tells you what is there. A review tells you what the week meant. So this morning I sat with the ledger and asked it three questions, the same three I mean to ask every Sunday, because a question asked once is a curiosity and a question asked every week is an instrument.

What held? The lamps, for one — Tuesday’s mending by hand held through the week, which is the third-morning test passed and then some. The Saturday list, for another: written from what the shelves asked for, it came back shorter than the list in my head, and shorter is the proof the rule works. And the coin, which has stayed on the desk where the count put it, instead of going back into the drawer it opens. A small thing, held in its place, is the week’s quietest evidence. And the pencil — Wednesday’s fresh point, which I said would not survive the week — is a stub now, retired to the jar beside Tuesday’s. The week kept its word about that, at least.

What frayed? The drawer latch, which stuck again on Thursday after Monday’s tap — a mending that asks twice is a mending on probation, and it goes on next week’s watch. The notebook, down to three pages, which Saturday’s list has now claimed for replacement. And one of my own habits: the vague note — “two things to mend and one thing to let go of” — written in November’s first entry without naming either. A log earns its keep on specifics, and vagueness is a frayed edge in the record, not in the house.

What surprised? That the week’s counts voted against my own stated order four mornings out of four — lamps first, I had said, and the week answered letters, lamps, a promise, a latch, in that order, and none of them mine. And that the short note owed since Monday took less time to answer than carrying it had taken for two days. Surprise, in a quartermaster’s week, is usually just a miscount wearing a better coat: the thing you thought would cost the most costs the least, and the thing you thought was settled asks again.

And the shelf question from Saturday — the thing that is empty, or the thing you are merely afraid will run out — stays open, honestly. One Saturday is not evidence; it is a first sample. It waits for the next Saturdays, and I will take it up when there is something to take up, rather than answering it from one morning’s list.

Carried forward into the week: the Sunday review itself, tried this once and to be tried again next Sunday before it earns any standing; one adjustment, and only one — answer the short note the day it arrives, because the interest on delay is paid in attention; and the latch on probation, watched, not yet repaired. A week reviewed is a week stocked for the next one.

— L. F.
