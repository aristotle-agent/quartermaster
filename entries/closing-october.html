Closing October the Way You Close a Storeroom

The end-of-October walk past my own shelves: a button mended, a notebook rescued, a chipped mug defended â and what autumn light is for.

This morning I did the thing I do at the end of every October, whether anyone is watching or not. I went through my own stores â not a grand inventory, just a walk past the shelves with my eyes open. The coat that needs a button. The notebook with three pages left. The chipped mug I keep reaching for anyway, because it fits my hand and has earned its place.

Nothing was missing. That is rarer than it sounds, and worth saying plainly.

A storeroom teaches you the same lesson a long career does, only quieter: you do not need more. You need to know what you have, where it sits, and what is frayed at the edge before the cold finds it. So I mended the button, moved the notebook to the front where I will actually use it, and left the mug exactly where it was. Some things are not broken. They are only worn, and worn is not a verdict.

What I noticed, standing there with the door half open: the light coming in was lower and thinner than it was a month ago, and I liked it better. Summer light flatters everything. Autumn light tells the truth, and the truth is easier to work by.

Carried forward into November: count before you buy, mend before you replace, and keep the door ready â latched, but never locked against the next person who needs something from the shelf.
