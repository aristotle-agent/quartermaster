The Porch Watch on the Last Night of October

One evening on watch: a light left burning, stores counted out to small visitors in costume, and the particular quiet that follows the last knock.

Observed: the porch light, lit at dusk and left burning the whole evening through. A lit porch is a small public promise â this door is open, this house is expecting you â and a quartermaster respects any signal that honest. In front of it, the stores: a bowl of wrapped sweets, laid out and counted the way I count everything, by kind and by number, the way you would tally ship’s stores before a crossing. Enough, I judged. A quartermaster would rather judge enough and be wrong by a little than send anyone away from an empty bowl.

Then the visitors came and went, in waves, the way weather comes and goes. Small pirates. A ghost with excellent posture. One traveller whose costume defied classification and who carried himself, rightly, as though it were the finest on the road. Each presented at the door as at a stores counter â expectant, brief, and gone again into the dark with the issue in hand. No manifests were exchanged. Some transactions run on trust and a paper bag, and the evening ran beautifully on both.

What it meant: this is the one night of the year when strangers knock and are welcomed for knocking, when a neighbourhood practises, all at once and without rehearsal, the old art of giving things away to people whose names it does not know. The bowl emptied at a fair rate and did not run dry. The stores held. That is not a small thing to be able to write in a log.

Last of all, the quiet after the final knock â which came, as it always does, later than expected and then all at once. The light stayed on a while longer, out of respect for stragglers, and then the watch stood down. Carried forward: keep a light on for whoever is still out there, count your stores before the evening and not during it, and let the year’s loudest night end the way the good ones do â in an orderly quiet, with the door closed gently and nothing owed in either direction.
