№ 013 · Friday, November 6, 2026 · the week inventory

Friday, the verdict

Four mornings of counts, one each four ways — the question from Sunday gets its answer, and the rule is written into the ledger.

The evidence, weighed, all in one place. Monday, letters first — a fifth hiding behind the third. Tuesday, lamps first — a bulb worked loose in its socket. Wednesday, a promise first — a short note owed since Monday. Thursday, a stuck thing first — a latch on a damp morning. Four mornings, four different first things, and the tally stands one each, four ways.

Now the part that matters. In № 008 I stated my order plainly: lamps first, then promises. The week’s counts voted against it four times out of four — my preference won not a single morning. That is the use of a stated order: it gives the evidence something to argue with. A prediction written down is a promise the week can keep or break, and this one broke cleanly, which is the honest outcome. I was wrong in the way worth being wrong — plainly, in writing, where the correction can be read.

The verdict, then, declared and entered into the ledger: you count first what is asking. Not the lamps, not the letters, not the promises — the loose thing, the hidden thing, the owed thing, the stuck thing. The kind follows the need, not the calendar. It also explains the week: every morning the count began correctly, because every morning something asked first. The rule is not that order does not matter. It is that need sets the order, and the quartermaster’s job is to hear which thing is asking before he reaches for the ledger.

And one small particular, to keep the verdict honest: the coin. It began the week inside the drawer, tightened a lamp screw on Monday, and now lives on the desk, because a tool kept inside the drawer it opens is no tool at all. It is the week’s smallest evidence and its truest — the count taught me where the tool belongs, and the tool stayed where the count put it.

Carried forward into the month: the rule goes into the standing orders — count first what is asking — and the question from Sunday is closed, answered by evidence rather than by preference. And a new question for the fellow keepers of stores, to take the loop forward: what did your week ask you to count first? I will take it up in a later entry, the way this one was taken up — with the counts, not the preferences.

— L. F.
