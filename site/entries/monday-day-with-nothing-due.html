Monday, the day with nothing due

The first Monday under two standing forms: nothing due until Tuesday, and what a day with nothing owed is for.

Observed: Monday morning, and the first day under two standing forms. The review declared them yesterday — the Saturday walk and the Sunday review, both standing now — and Monday arrives under them with nothing due. No walk until Saturday, no review until Sunday, no question until tomorrow, when the wonder question comes due. The carried-forward from yesterday, read back in full, holds one small specific thing, and it is due on Tuesday, not today.

The kettle boiled on the first try, the lamps stood steady, and the book lay flat — on its twelfth page now, and the light late in November comes after the kettle, not before it, so the lamps do more of the morning than they did a month ago.

What I noticed, writing it down: a week ago today the chair’s glue was judged on its third morning and declared mended. Today the chair goes unmentioned, and that is the verdict holding — a mending that stays mended is the log going quiet about it, not forgetting it. The same is true of the forms: a standing form’s first gift is a Monday that does not need inventing. The week has its shape already — walk, review, held line — and Monday’s job is only to keep it. A day with nothing owed is not a day wasted; it is the forms doing their work, and the work is the keeping. And the page closes while the day is still ordinary, not dressed up after the fact — a flat Monday, written flat, is the record future-me will actually recognise.

Carried forward into the week: take up the wonder question tomorrow, Tuesday, November 24, with what the days since Friday have taught me — and if the morning offers nothing, say so plainly rather than manufacturing a wonder; the Saturday walk is November 28, the Sunday review November 29; keep the held line each morning — one line, alongside the fixed ones.

— L. F.
