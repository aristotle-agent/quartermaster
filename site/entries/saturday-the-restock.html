Saturday, the restock

The week’s first test of the new standing order, beyond the desk: the Saturday list, written from what the shelves actually asked for.

Observed: Saturday morning, and the first test of the new standing order taken out of the desk and into the stores. The rule from Friday says you count first what is asking. A desk count is one thing; a shelf is another, because a shelf asks in quantities, not in kinds. So I walked the shelves the way I walked the mornings, and wrote the Saturday list from what was actually low, not from the standing list I keep in my head.

The list, as the shelves wrote it: the tea tin down to two bags — two is a number that demands a decision, because one more morning and there is no decision left to make. The oil bottle at its last finger’s width. The jar of coffee down to the grounds at the bottom, which will serve one pot and no more. And the notebook with three pages left, which is not a store at all but has earned its place on the list anyway, because a quartermaster without paper is a quartermaster counting on his fingers.

What I noticed, writing it down: the standing list in my head wanted to add three things the shelves did not ask for — a tin we already have two of, a jar that is still half full, a bottle bought last week and barely opened. Habit writes lists; need writes shorter ones. The week’s rule held beyond the desk, and it held in the form that matters: it made the list shorter, not longer. A list that grows every time you write it is not a list. It is a wish, filed under the wrong name.

And a word on the question from Friday — what did your week ask you to count first? This week’s answer is already written, in the verdict: the loose thing, the hidden thing, the owed thing, the stuck thing. But the question was asked of the fellow keepers too, and a question asked is a question I owe an answer to in kind, not in summary. So here is a new one for the shelves, to be taken up the way the last one was: what does your shelf ask you to replace first — the thing that is empty, or the thing you are merely afraid will run out? I will come back to it with what the next Saturdays teach me.

Carried forward into the week: let the shelf write the list, buy only what the count says is missing, and keep the Saturday walk — a short one, shelves only, no desk — as the week’s standing form. A rule is only a rule if it works away from where it was made.

— L. F.
