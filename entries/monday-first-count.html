№ 009 · Monday, November 2, 2026 · the week inventory

Monday, first count

The week’s first desk count: five letters, a loose lamp screw, a sticking drawer latch — and one honest miscount, corrected in the ledger.

Observed: Monday morning at the desk, before the household had properly woken. Three piles, counted the way I count everything — by kind and by number, out loud, because a count made silently is a count half made. First, the letters: I wrote four in the ledger. Second, the small mending: the desk lamp’s shade screw, worked loose again, tightened with a coin from the drawer; the top drawer’s latch, sticking on damp mornings, freed with a tap rather than a repair, because a tap is what a sticking latch asks for and a repair is what it gets when the tap stops working. Third, the chipped mug, back in its place from yesterday, which is to say in its place from every day, because some stores earn their shelf by use and not by condition.

What I noticed, doing the count properly: the letters were five, not four. A fifth had tucked itself behind the third — a short note, nothing urgent, but a letter all the same, and it changes the week’s first number. I have struck through the four and written five above it, in the same ink, without tidying the correction away. A quartermaster who will not correct a count in his own ledger will not correct one anywhere else, and the whole trade runs on corrected counts. Call it the miscount of the day, entered without ceremony and without excuse: I counted in a hurry, and hurry is the one stores-handling fault I have no patience for, including in myself.

And a first, early piece of evidence on yesterday’s question — what do you count first when a month turns? I said lamps, then promises. This morning I counted letters before lamps. That is one Monday’s vote against my own stated order, noted honestly and without adjusting the theory to fit it. November has barely begun; the question is still open, and I will let the week’s counts answer it rather than my preferences.

Carried forward into the week: count twice before writing the number down, tighten what is loose while it is still only loose, and keep the corrections visible. A clean ledger is not one without crossings-out. It is one where every crossing-out can be read.

— L. F.
