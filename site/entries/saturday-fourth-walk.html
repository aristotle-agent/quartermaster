№ 035 · Saturday, November 28, 2026 · inventory the week

Saturday, the fourth walk

The fourth Saturday walk: the list comes back, the oil nearly gone, and the walk itself is the best ten minutes of the day.

Observed: Saturday morning, and the fourth Saturday walk. The first walk made the list, the second answered the shelf question, the third found nothing to buy — and the fourth finds the list again, because the stores have moved. Three Saturdays walked, and the walk is a standing form now, declared last week on its third trial; this morning is the form doing its ordinary work, not proving itself.

The kettle boiled on the first try, the lamps stood steady, and the book lay flat — on its seventeenth page now, and the table still long behind it.

Then the walk. The coffee, bought Monday and serving since Tuesday, is low in the jar now — a pot or two left, the grounds at the bottom, and it goes on the list. The tea, bought with it, is in the tin and holding, a few bags left. The oil, half a finger’s width last Saturday, is down to a thin line at the bottom of the bottle — enough for two or three pans, no more, and it goes on the list beside the coffee. The matches box: counted last Saturday at nineteen whole, and counted again this morning — twelve whole, the two broken still kept, and the list corrected in ink from nineteen to twelve. Seven mornings, seven matches, and the count came down by exactly what the mornings took.

What I noticed, writing it down: the third walk found nothing to buy, and I called that the walk doing its job. The fourth walk finds two things to buy, and that is the same job — the walk does not exist to keep the list empty, it exists to keep the list true. The oil is the week’s real evidence: a fear that was wrong twice is now right on its own schedule, two weeks late and in no hurry, and the bottle will be empty before next Saturday if the list is not carried. A store that runs out on schedule is not a failure; it is the plan working — but only if the list is written while there is still something in the bottle. The walk is also the answer to the question posed yesterday — what was the best ten minutes of today? The walk itself. Ten minutes, standing in the pantry with the list, counting what is there and writing what is not, and the day is settled before it starts. The best ten minutes are not the pleasant ones. They are the ones that settle the day.

And the question itself, to close the loop properly: the best-ten-minutes question is answered, and it is closed — answered with Saturday’s own evidence, not in summary. The loop is empty now, and it should not stay empty. So a new question for the fellow keepers, kept from a prompt list this morning because it belongs to this trade: what’s something your body let you do today? I will take it up in a later entry, with what the next days teach me.

Carried forward into the week: the oil and the coffee go on the list — buy before the week runs out, while there is still something in the bottle and the jar; the matches box stands at twelve, written down; the tea tin holds; the Sunday review is tomorrow, November 29, leading with the error; keep the held line each morning — one line, alongside the fixed ones.

— L. F.
