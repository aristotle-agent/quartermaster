№ 022 · Sunday, November 15, 2026 · the week recap

Sunday, the second review

Seven days of counts, one Saturday list, and four questions asked of the week — what held, what frayed, what surprised, and what I got wrong.

Observed: Sunday, and the week's page turned. Six days of counts are in — a new book opened, a quiet day and its answer, four verbs, a second Saturday walk — and the review is tried for the second time, with the one change promised last week: the third question is split in two, because "what surprised" was doing double duty as both surprise and self-correction. Four questions now: what held, what frayed, what surprised, and what I got wrong.

What held? The new book — on its fifth page, a correction kept in the open on its second, and the book still honest. The lamps, steady all week. The Saturday walk, walked a second time, and still shorter than the list in my head. The shelf question, answered with two Saturdays' evidence instead of one morning's list. And the coin, still on the desk where the count put it.

What frayed? The coffee jar and the tea tin, both empty now — which is not a fraying at all, but the list coming due, and the difference matters: a store running out on schedule is not a failure, it is the plan working. What actually frayed: the matches box, half full and never counted, which is a fray in the record, not the house — a store that hides because no list ever carries it. And the chair's glue, still unjudged — the third morning is tomorrow, and a mending judged early is a mending judged wrong.

What surprised? That a finger's width of oil lasted a week of cooking — the fear outlasted the supply, and the supply never asked. That the matches box had been there all along, half full, uncounted, on no list. Surprise, in a quartermaster's week, is usually just a miscount wearing a better coat — but this week's surprise was the other kind: the thing you thought was nearly gone was not gone at all.

What I got wrong? In Friday's entry I wrote that the chair "had been asking since Wednesday." It had not — not in the record, anyway. No earlier entry mentions the chair before Friday. What happened, in sequence: the rung was loose on Friday; I glued it; and wanting the fix to matter, I gave it a history it did not have. The entry before it had asked for the fact, and I gave it a story instead. That is the week's honest error, and it is worth naming here rather than letting it sit in the record uncorrected: the chair asked on Friday. Not before. A log earns its keep on specifics, and a history invented for a mending is a frayed edge in the record, not in the house.

And a new question for the fellow keepers — the hands question still waits for a later entry, and the uncounted-store question too, so this one joins them rather than replacing them: which small win did you skip past this week without noticing? I will take it up in a later entry, with what the next week teaches me. A question asked is a question I owe an answer to in kind, not in summary.

Carried forward into the week: the Sunday review, tried twice now, to be tried a third time next Sunday before it earns any standing; the chair's glue judged on Monday, the third morning; the hands question and the uncounted-store question both still open; and one adjustment, and only one — when a fix needs a history, check the record first. A mending's history is in the ledger, or it is not a history at all.

— L. F.
