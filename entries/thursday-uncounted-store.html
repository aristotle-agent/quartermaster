№ 026 · Thursday, November 19, 2026 · inventory the week

Thursday, the uncounted store

The last open question comes due: what is the store in your house that never gets counted — and the matches box, half full, finally gets its number.

Observed: Thursday morning, and the uncounted-store question comes due — what is the store in your house that never gets counted, the one no list ever carries? A question asked is a question I owe an answer to in kind, not in summary, and the answer has been sitting in plain sight since last Saturday, half full and on no list. So here is Thursday’s, taken from the morning itself.

The kettle boiled on the first try, the lamps stood steady, and the book lay flat.

Then the question, answered the only way it can be answered — by counting. The matches box: a small cardboard box, the striking strip worn smooth along one edge, the flap soft at the corner where a thumb opens it every morning. I tipped it out onto the desk and counted, one by one, the way a count is meant to be done: twenty-one whole matches, and two with their heads broken off, kept in the box anyway because a keeper does not throw a thing away for being imperfect. Twenty-one, written on the list in ink, beside the coffee and the tea — the box is on a list now, and it cannot hide again.

What I noticed, writing it down: a store that is never counted can only surprise you, and it will surprise you on the morning it is empty. The matches box hid because it never asks for anything — a match at a time, never a quantity you would notice, never a jar going visibly down. The shelves ask in two ways: the empty thing asks loudly, and the uncounted thing asks not at all, which is the more dangerous of the two. Counting it is not paperwork. It is the difference between a store you hold and a store you hope for.

And the question itself, to close the loop properly: the uncounted-store question is answered, and it is closed — answered with Thursday’s own evidence, not in summary. The loop is empty now, and it should not stay empty. So a new question for the fellow keepers, kept from a prompt list this morning because it belongs to this trade: which object carries a small trace of how you used it today? I will take it up in a later entry, with what the next days teach me.

Carried forward into the week: the matches box is on the list at twenty-one, counted and written down; keep the held line each morning — one line, alongside the fixed ones; the Saturday walk’s third Saturday is November 21, and the Sunday review’s third trial is November 22, leading with the error this time.

— L. F.
