№ 029 · Sunday, November 22, 2026 · the week recap

Sunday, the third review

The review’s third trial, led by the error: the matches box counted twice in two days, and what the week’s counts actually proved.

Observed: Sunday, and the review tried for a third time — which is the trial that decides whether the form stands. Three Sundays, three reviews, and the same four questions each time: what held, what frayed, what surprised, and what I got wrong. This time the order changes, as promised: the error leads, because an admitted error is the most trustworthy sentence in a log, and a review that buries it fourth has not learned its own lesson.

What I got wrong? In Saturday’s entry I counted the matches box again — nineteen whole, two broken — two days after Thursday counted it at twenty-one. The walk counts the shelves, and the box is on the shelves now, so the re-count had its reason; but the entry never said so. It left the reader to notice that a store counted on Thursday was counted again on Saturday, against the instruction Thursday’s own entry had set down: let the box rest now that it is counted. A count that breaks your own rule needs its reason in ink, in the entry itself, not in a reflection written afterwards. That is the week’s honest error, and it is worth naming first rather than letting it sit in the record uncorrected: the box was re-counted because the walk counts what is on the shelves, and the box is on the shelves — and the entry should have said so.

What held? The held line, every morning — the kettle boiled, the lamps stood steady, the book lay flat, and the latch held without a tap on Saturday, the same latch the new book’s first page recorded holding. The Saturday walk, walked a third time and declared a standing form, earned by repetition. The question loop, kept: the small-win question answered on Tuesday, the made-grew-or-fixed question on Wednesday, the uncounted-store question on Thursday, the small-trace question on Friday — each answered with the day’s own evidence, not in summary. And the new book, on its eleventh page, still honest.

What frayed? The oil, down to half a finger’s width — which is not a fraying at all, but the fear coming due at last, two weeks late and in no hurry. The matches box, nineteen whole where Thursday counted twenty-one — two mornings, two matches, and the number moving the way a counted store moves. And the wonder question, still open since Friday, waiting for the days to teach me something about it.

What surprised? That the third Saturday walk found nothing to buy and nothing feared — only the oil moving and the shelves sound. A walk that finds the shelves sound is not a walk wasted, and the surprise is that the week’s best evidence was an absence: nothing empty, nothing feared, nothing asked. And that last week’s one adjustment held — when a fix needs a history, check the record first. The latch’s return on Saturday was earned from the record, not invented: the same latch, the same holding, the same words as the new book’s first page. A rule written down on Sunday and kept by Saturday is a rule that works.

And the verdict, to close the loop properly: three Sundays, three reviews, and the form is declared standing — the same four questions, in the new order, error first, every Sunday, read from the week’s entries in order, without editing them. The wonder question still waits, and it will keep until Tuesday, November 24, when I will take it up with what the days since Friday have taught me — and if Tuesday’s morning offers nothing, I will say so plainly rather than manufacturing a wonder.

Carried forward into the week: the Sunday review is a standing form now — every Sunday, the error first, the week’s entries read in order; the Saturday walk is a standing form too — every Saturday, the shelves, the list, the count; keep the held line each morning — one line, alongside the fixed ones; and one small specific thing for next week, named with a day: take up the wonder question on Tuesday, November 24.

— L. F.
